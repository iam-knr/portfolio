Pick your first use case with a simple score

Ten use cases is a long list. You will not build ten. Score each one on four questions, then start with the highest total. Give each question 1 to 5 points.

Question1 point means5 points means
How often does the task repeat?A few times a yearEvery day
How clear is the right answer?Pure opinionYou can check it against data
How bad is a mistake?Money or customers harmedYou catch it before anyone notices
How ready is the data?Scattered, no accessOne source, clean, with access

Reporting and search term review usually score 17 to 20. Lead follow up scores lower on mistake cost, because a customer reads the message, so it starts in draft mode. Budget changes score lowest. Leave those for last.

SOP: Lead response agent, from form to first reply

This is the use case most Indian businesses ask me about first, because speed to first reply decides a lot of sales. Here is the procedure I use.

SOP 2: Lead response agent Owner: marketing lead. Setup: 2 to 4 weeks including the draft period

  1. Map the current path. Write down what happens from form submit to first human call today, with real times. Take ten recent leads and note the gap for each.
  2. Set the target. Example: first reply within 2 minutes, qualified lead flagged to sales within 10 minutes.
  3. Collect consent. The form must state that the person agrees to be contacted on WhatsApp and phone. Store the consent time. Use approved message templates only.
  4. Build the intake. Trigger on new lead. Check for a duplicate in the CRM by phone and email. Stop if it is a duplicate and tag the old record instead.
  5. Add the classifier. The agent reads the form answers and labels intent: ready to buy, researching, not a fit. It must return the label and the sentence that supports it.
  6. Draft the first reply. Use a template with two or three fields filled by the agent, such as name and the product they asked about. Keep it short.
  7. Draft mode for two weeks. The reply goes to a person as a draft. They approve or edit. Log every edit. The edits tell you what to fix.
  8. Review the log. If a person changed fewer than 1 in 10 drafts, allow auto-send for the "ready to buy" label only.
  9. Add the alert. If a hot lead has no human follow up in 30 minutes, ping the sales owner.
  10. Measure monthly. Time to first reply, share of leads reaching a call, and cost per qualified lead.
Output: a working agent plus a log. Stop rule: if reply edits stay above 3 in 10 after four weeks, fix the templates and the intake questions before adding features.

Worked example: what the numbers could look like

Illustrative example. Not a client result.

A coaching business gets 400 leads a month. Today the first reply takes about 6 hours on average because the team checks forms twice a day. They want a reply in 2 minutes.

  • Baseline: 400 leads, 6 hour average first reply, 18 percent reach a sales call, so 72 calls.
  • After the agent: first reply in about 2 minutes. If the call rate rose to 22 percent, that is 88 calls, 16 more.
  • If one call in five becomes a sale, that is about 3 extra sales a month. Multiply by your average sale value to see if the build is worth it.

Treat the 22 percent as a guess you test, not a promise. Your own numbers decide. The point of the exercise is the arithmetic, not the figures.

SOP: Weekly ad account review

SOP 3: Weekly review Owner: media buyer. Time: 45 minutes with an agent, about 3 hours by hand

  1. Agent run, Monday 8 am. Pull last 7 days by campaign: cost, clicks, conversions, cost per conversion, budget spent by noon.
  2. Agent compares to targets. A sheet holds the target per campaign. Flag anything off by more than 20 percent.
  3. Agent adds causes. For each flag, list the top three possible causes from the data, such as a search term, an ad disapproval or a landing page error.
  4. Human reads the flags. Ignore noise, mark real issues.
  5. Human decides actions. Pause, change, test, or leave. Write the reason in one line.
  6. Log. Store the week, the flags, the decisions and the reasons. In a month you will see patterns.
Output: one page of flags and decisions. Never let the agent apply changes in the first month.

How to know it is working

Choose two numbers per agent, one for speed and one for quality. For lead response: time to first reply and share of leads reaching a call. For ad audits: hours saved per week and number of real issues found that you would have missed. Review them monthly. If neither number moves in two months, the problem is usually the data or the task choice, not the model.

Keep a short change log. Each time you edit the brief, write the date and why. Six months later it is the best training material your team will have.
