A page anatomy you can copy

Here is the order of sections I use for a lead page. Each block has one job. Remove a block if its job is not needed for your offer.

BlockJobRule of thumb
Headline and subheadRepeat the ad promiseUnder 12 words. One idea
Main actionLet them act without scrollingOne button, one verb
Proof stripReduce doubt fastReal logos, ratings with source, or a number you can defend
Three benefitsSay what they getOutcome first, feature second
How it worksMake the next step clearThree steps, plain words
Detailed proofCase, testimonial, photosOnly real, only permitted
FAQAnswer the last objectionsFive to seven questions from real calls
Final actionCatch people who scrolledRepeat the button and the promise

SOP: Build and launch a landing page

SOP 12: Page build and launch Owner: marketer with a developer for tracking. Time: 1 to 2 days

  1. Write the one sentence. For [who] who wants [result], we give [offer] without [fear].
  2. Collect proof. Gather real testimonials with written permission, real numbers with a source, and real photos.
  3. Collect questions. Pull the top ten questions from calls, chats and reviews.
  4. Outline. Use the anatomy above. Cut blocks you cannot fill honestly.
  5. Draft copy with a model. Ask for three versions. Edit into one voice. Delete unproven claims.
  6. Build the page. A coding agent can make a first version. Ask for plain HTML and CSS, set image sizes, and no heavy libraries.
  7. Review the code. Check forms, links, tracking snippets, and any added scripts you did not ask for.
  8. Add tracking. Fire the conversion on the thank you page only. Test that it records once.
  9. Test on devices. One low-cost Android phone on mobile data, one iPhone, one laptop.
  10. Test the lead path. Submit the form. Confirm the CRM entry, the reply message and the sales alert.
  11. Add privacy text. A short notice and a link to the full policy near the form.
  12. Soft launch. Send a small budget first. Watch for errors for 24 hours.
  13. Scale. Increase spend only after the lead path works end to end.
Output: a live page, a test log and a tracking proof. Stop rule: if any step of the lead path fails, pause traffic until fixed.

Message match: a worked example

Illustrative example

Ad: "Free site audit in 24 hours for online stores."

  • Weak page headline: "Digital solutions for your business." The visitor wonders if they clicked the wrong thing.
  • Strong page headline: "Free site audit for your online store, in 24 hours." The promise repeats.
  • Subhead: "Send your store link. You get a short report with the three fixes that matter most."
  • Button: "Get my free audit". Not "Submit".

The same visitor, the same offer, a different result, because the page confirms they are in the right place.

Form design rules

  • Ask only what you need for the first reply: name, phone, one qualifying question.
  • Use the right input types so phones show number keys.
  • Label fields above the box. Do not rely on grey placeholder text.
  • Show clear error text next to the field.
  • State what happens next: "We will call within 30 minutes during working hours."
  • Do not pre-tick consent boxes.

Speed checklist

Most speed loss comes from large images, many scripts and slow fonts. Fix those three first. Test again after each fix so you know which change helped.

SOP: A/B test on a landing page

SOP 13: Landing page test Owner: marketer. Cycle: 2 to 4 weeks

  1. Pick one question. Example: does a shorter form raise qualified leads?
  2. Write the rule before the test. Metric, minimum leads per version, and the day you will decide.
  3. Split traffic evenly. Same source, same time, random split.
  4. Change one thing. Not headline and form together.
  5. Do not peek and stop early. Wait for the planned volume.
  6. Judge by qualified leads, not form fills alone.
  7. Log the result. Keep losing tests too. They teach as much.
Output: a one line lesson per test. If traffic is too low for a clean test, compare before and after for a month and be honest that it is weaker evidence.
