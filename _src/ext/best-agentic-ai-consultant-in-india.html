SOP: Run your own consultant selection

SOP 14: Selecting an agentic AI consultant Owner: business owner or operations head. Time: 2 to 3 weeks

  1. Write the problem, not the solution. "Our leads wait six hours for a reply" is a problem. "We need an AI agent" is a guess.
  2. Write the number. What would success look like in hours saved, reply time, or cost per qualified lead?
  3. List your systems. CRM, ad accounts, WhatsApp provider, sheets, email. The consultant must work with these.
  4. Shortlist three. One independent, one boutique studio, one other type. This compares models, not only people.
  5. Send the same brief to all three. One page: problem, number, systems, timeline, and what data is sensitive.
  6. Hold a 45 minute call with each. Use the nine questions above. Take notes in a shared sheet.
  7. Ask for a live demo or recorded walkthrough. Real data, real errors, no slides.
  8. Request a written pilot proposal. Scope, steps, timeline, price, what you own, how it ends.
  9. Score each one. Use the table below. Weigh evidence over charm.
  10. Check references. Speak to one past client if possible.
  11. Sign a short pilot. Two to four weeks. Review before anything longer.
Output: a scored comparison and a signed pilot. Stop rule: if a consultant will not show anything real, move on.

What to put in the contract

  • Scope. A list of what will be built and what will not.
  • Success number. Written and measurable.
  • Ownership. You own workflows, prompts, documents and accounts. Accounts are created in your name.
  • Access. Limited keys, removed at the end. A list of who had access.
  • Data. What is stored, where, for how long, and deletion at the end.
  • Costs. Build fee, hosting, model usage, support. Each priced separately.
  • Handover. Documentation, a training session, a recorded walkthrough.
  • Exit. How either side can end the work and what happens to the files.
  • Support. Hours, response times, who is on call.

I am not a lawyer. Have a lawyer read the final agreement, especially for data and liability terms.

A sample pilot plan

Illustrative plan. Adjust to your case.
  • Week 0. Kickoff. Access to the CRM in read-only mode. Agree the success number.
  • Week 1. Map the current process with timings. Design the agent on paper. Review together.
  • Week 2. Build the read-only version. Test on last month's data. Compare with the manual result.
  • Week 3. Add draft mode with approvals. Run on live leads. Log every edit.
  • Week 4. Review the numbers. Write the decision: keep, widen or stop. Hand over documents.

Questions to ask about security and data

  • Which model provider will see our data, and under what terms?
  • Is any data used to train models?
  • Where are the servers? Who can access them?
  • How are keys stored and rotated?
  • What is logged, and for how long?
  • What happens to our data when the project ends?
  • How do you protect against prompt injection from emails or web pages the agent reads?

When you should not hire a consultant yet

  • Your tracking and CRM are a mess. Fix the data first.
  • Nobody on your side can own the system after handover.
  • You cannot name one task and one number.
  • The process itself is broken. Automating a bad process makes it fail faster.

In these cases a short audit is a better first purchase than a build.
