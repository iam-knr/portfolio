Where AI fits in the funnel

A funnel is a way to see where people drop off. AI can help at each stage, but the help is different. Here is the map I use when a business asks "where do we start?"

StageQuestionAI helpHuman job
AwarenessDo the right people see us?Creative variants, audience research, content briefsChoose message and channel
InterestDo they click and read?Landing page checks, headline testsDecide offer and proof
DecisionDo they enquire?Lead scoring, instant reply draftsHandle price and objections
PurchaseDo they buy?Follow up reminders, call summariesClose the sale
RetentionDo they come back?Churn flags, reply drafts, review monitoringFix the cause of churn

Most teams jump to awareness because creative is fun. The money is often at decision and retention, where a fast reply or a clean follow up matters more than a new ad.

SOP: A 30 day AI pilot for a marketing team

SOP 8: 30 day pilot Owner: head of marketing. Team time: 2 hours a week

  1. Week 1. Time audit. Ask each person to list weekly tasks and the hours each takes for two weeks. Do not guess.
  2. Week 1. Pick two tasks. Choose repeated, checkable tasks, such as weekly reports and first draft copy. Avoid anything customer-facing for now.
  3. Week 1. Set the baseline. Write the current hours and error rate for each task.
  4. Week 2. Write the rules. One page: what data may go into AI tools, what must never go in (customer lists, contracts, passwords), who approves outputs.
  5. Week 2. Build the prompts. Save the best prompt for each task in a shared doc with an example output.
  6. Week 3. Run in parallel. Do the task the old way and the new way. Compare time and quality.
  7. Week 4. Decide. If the new way saves at least a third of the time with no drop in quality, adopt it for that task.
  8. Week 4. Write the lesson. One page of what worked, what failed and the next two tasks.
Output: two adopted tasks, a prompt library, a data rule sheet. Stop rule: if quality drops, keep the human version and try a smaller piece of the task.

Prompt templates that I actually reuse

Research summary You are helping a marketing team. Here are 40 customer reviews. Group them by theme. For each theme give a name, how many reviews mention it, and two exact quotes. List themes in order of count. Do not add themes that are not in the reviews.
Ad copy angles Product: [one line]. Audience: [who, where]. Offer: [what, price if allowed]. Write 5 primary text options, each with a different angle: price, speed, trust, proof, fear of missing out. Label each by angle. Maximum 40 words each. No claims that need proof I have not given.
Report note Here are this week's numbers [table]. Write a note for a business owner in plain words. Three parts: what moved, the likely reason from the data, one action. If the data does not show the reason, say so.

All three share a pattern: give the data, say who reads it, set a shape for the answer, and tell the model what to do when it does not know. That last line saves you from many confident wrong answers.

Data rules you should write before anyone uses a tool

  • Never paste customer phone numbers, emails, payment details, passwords or signed contracts into a public AI tool.
  • Anonymize when you need help with real data. Replace names and numbers with labels.
  • Check the tool's terms on whether your inputs are used to train models. Business plans often differ from free plans.
  • Keep client data inside tools your client agreement allows. Ask if unsure.
  • India context. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets duties on handling personal data. Take professional advice on what applies to you.

Measuring AI work honestly

People love to say "AI saved us 10 hours." Ask how they know. Here is a simple method.

  1. Time the task by hand three times and take the average.
  2. Time the AI-assisted version three times, including your checking time.
  3. Compare error rates by checking ten outputs from each.
  4. Count only the net gain. A fast draft that needs two hours of repair is not a saving.

Three small stories of where AI went wrong

These are patterns I have seen or been told about, described in general terms, not about named clients.

  • The invented statistic. A blog draft included a percentage that sounded right. Nobody checked it. It was wrong and was later quoted by someone else. Fix: every number needs a source link before publishing.
  • The off-brand reply. An auto-reply was friendly but promised a refund policy that did not exist. Fix: replies use fixed templates and the model only fills names and product.
  • The tracking tag. A generated landing page changed a tracking snippet and conversions were not recorded for a week. Fix: test conversion tracking on every page change.
