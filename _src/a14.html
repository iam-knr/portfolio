What programmatic SEO is

Programmatic SEO is a way to build many search pages without writing each one by hand. You keep a table of data, such as cities, products or services. You write one template. A tool fills the template once for each row.

The classic examples are "plumber in [city]", "[tool] vs [tool]" and "best [thing] for [use]". The search patterns repeat, so the pages can share a layout.

The idea is old. What changed is how easy it is to make a thousand pages, and how easy it is for a search engine to spot a thousand pages that say nothing.

When it works and when it hurts

It works when a person searching for that exact thing would be glad to land on the page. A page for "digital marketing course in Kochi" should say something true about Kochi that a page about Pune would not.

It hurts when the template does all the talking. If only the city name changes, you have one page copied many times. Search engines treat that as thin content. In the worst case, it can pull down the pages that were good.

My test is simple. Open two generated pages next to each other. If you can swap the headings and nobody would notice, they are not different enough.

The rules I use for every generated page

  1. Each page answers its own search. If two pages would give the same answer, make one page.
  2. Every row of data has real facts. Places, prices, steps, examples. No invented numbers, quotes or reviews.
  3. At least 40% of the text is unique to the page. Boilerplate is fine in a template. It cannot be most of the page.
  4. Put a short answer first. Then the detail, then a few real questions people ask, in a visible FAQ.
  5. One H1, one canonical, a different title and description on every page. Check the template does not output two canonicals.
  6. Link the pages together on purpose. A pillar page links to each child, each child links back, and related pages link to each other.
  7. Give each page a next step. A form, a free download or a call booking. A page with no next step wastes the visit.
  8. Launch in small batches. Read 10 pages by hand first. Publish 10 to 20, watch indexing, then add more. Keep weak pages out of the index until they are fixed.

Where AI helps and where it should not

AI is useful at three steps of this work, and I use it carefully. This is the same pattern I describe in agentic AI in marketing.

  • Preparing the data. An AI tool can clean a messy table, find missing fields and suggest extra columns. A person checks the result.
  • Drafting the unique parts. It can write a first version of the city or product specific paragraph from the facts you gave it. You edit it. It must not add facts you did not provide.
  • Quality checks. A workflow can scan every generated page for repeated sentences, missing sections, double titles and broken links. n8n is a good fit for this kind of repeat check.

I would not let an agent publish a batch on its own. Publishing is the step where a person says yes. That rule comes up in every AI automation job I do, and it matters more when the output is hundreds of public pages.

Pages also need to convert. Traffic to a page with no clear offer is wasted, so read my guide on AI landing pages before you build the template.

How to measure whether it worked

  • Group the pages and look at impressions and clicks for the group in Search Console. One page tells you little.
  • Check how many pages are indexed after two weeks. A big gap between published and indexed means the pages may look thin.
  • Count leads from the pages, not only visits.
  • After two months, merge or remove pages that get no impressions.
  • Refresh the facts every quarter. Change the date only when the content really changed.

I do not promise a ranking for any page. Search engines change how they treat generated content, so check current guidance before you build at scale.

Frequently asked questions

What is programmatic SEO?

Programmatic SEO is making many pages from a data file and one template, where each page answers a different search. Examples are city pages, comparison pages and product pages.

Is programmatic SEO bad for rankings?

It can be if the pages are thin or near copies of each other. It can work when each page has real facts that differ from the others and is useful to the reader.

How many pages should I publish at once?

Start with 10 to 20 after reading a sample by hand. Watch indexing for two weeks, then add more in batches.

How much of each page should be unique?

My rule for generated pages is at least 40% unique text per page. Boilerplate in the template is fine, but it should not be most of the page.

Can AI write programmatic SEO pages?

It can draft the unique parts from facts you provide, and it can check pages for repeated text and errors. A person should review the output, and no agent should publish a whole batch without approval.

What do I need before I start?

Good data and a good template. A tool only builds the pages. The plugin page on WordPress.org lists what the plugin needs and how it works.
